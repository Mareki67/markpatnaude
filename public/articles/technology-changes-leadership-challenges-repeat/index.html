Technology Changes. Leadership Challenges Repeat.

A leadership framework for navigating technology change.

By Mark Patnaude • 2026 • Originally published on LinkedIn

Every major technology wave follows a familiar pattern.

Adoption.

Growth.

Complexity.

Governance.

AI isn't changing the pattern. It's compressing the timeline. It's easy to focus on what AI can do.

Technology changes quickly. Organizations don't always change at the same pace.

I've watched organizations navigate multiple technology transitions throughout my career. Whether modernizing infrastructure, implementing enterprise systems, enabling remote work, or adopting emerging technologies, every wave created new opportunities to make organizations more capable.

The technology changed, but the leadership questions did not.

Adoption

Every technology wave begins with opportunity.

Organizations naturally begin asking how a new technology can help the business. Can it make work easier? Help people make better decisions? Create business value?

That curiosity is healthy because innovation should begin with solving a business problem, not adopting technology for its own sake.

The first leadership question should not be:

How do we govern this?

It should be:

What business problem are we trying to solve?

Without a clear business purpose, even successful technology initiatives can struggle to deliver meaningful business value.

Growth

As adoption grows, the leadership challenge changes.

Success encourages broader adoption. New use cases emerge, more people become involved, and existing platforms continue to evolve.

What began as a focused initiative becomes part of everyday business.

That's when leadership begins asking different questions.

  • Who owns the outcome?
  • How will success be measured?
  • What level of investment is justified?

Those answers help organizations continue growing without creating unnecessary complexity.

Complexity

Success often introduces complexity.

As organizations become more capable, they also become more complex.

More platforms, more vendors, more information, and more operational dependencies make it harder to maintain visibility and make consistent decisions.

Many leaders experienced this progression during the rapid shift to remote work.

The first priority was enabling people to work. As those environments matured, attention naturally shifted toward cybersecurity, identity management, support, compliance, and operational consistency.

Recent research reinforces this pattern. Organizations are adopting AI rapidly, yet many continue to report limited visibility into AI software spending, increasing concern over unnecessary AI-related costs, and gaps in formal governance.

Together, those patterns point to a leadership challenge, not just a technology challenge.

Governance

Good governance allows innovation to scale safely.

Some people see governance as slowing innovation.

My experience has been different.

Good governance provides the structure organizations need to turn experimentation into consistent business results.

Governance is not simply an IT responsibility. It is an organizational responsibility.

  • Technology implements solutions.
  • Security helps manage risk.
  • Finance evaluates investment.
  • Legal advises on compliance.
  • Leadership owns the outcomes.

AI is compressing the timeline. Organizations are moving from adoption to governance in months rather than years.

The leadership questions arrive much sooner.

Questions Every Leader Should Ask

Whether evaluating AI or the next wave of emerging technology, I believe the leadership questions remain remarkably consistent.

  • What business problem are we trying to solve?
  • How will success be measured?
  • What level of risk is acceptable for our organization?
  • Who owns the outcome?
  • What controls, ownership, and measures need to mature as adoption grows?

Those questions are not intended to slow innovation.

They help connect innovation to business value.

Final Thoughts

The organizations that create lasting value from AI may not be the ones that adopt the most tools the fastest.

They may be the ones that help their organizations mature alongside the technology.

Technology changes. Leadership challenges repeat.

The only thing that changes is how quickly organizations must respond.
